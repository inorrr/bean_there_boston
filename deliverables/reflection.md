# Short Reflection

The data supports using Bean There Boston as an early screening tool for choosing neighborhoods to investigate further. It can show where public indicators point in the same direction: high density, strong transit access, a larger young/student population, higher-income households, walkability, parking access, and lower measured competition. Under the default assumptions, West End and Beacon Hill look especially promising because they combine several favorable signals at once. Downtown also performs well, but the competition proxy is much heavier there, which makes it a more uncertain recommendation.

The data does not prove that a cafe would be profitable in any neighborhood. It cannot show rent, lease availability, buildout costs, storefront visibility, true pedestrian traffic, zoning issues, financing, staffing, menu-market fit, or current competitor quality. Food licenses are only a proxy for competition, and the cafe-like category is based on text matching rather than a verified classification. Neighborhood-level averages can also hide very different block-by-block conditions.

The most responsible use of the site is to narrow the search. A user should take the top neighborhoods, visit specific corridors, compare rents, count foot traffic at different times, inspect nearby cafe menus and prices, and check zoning and lease constraints before making any business decision.
